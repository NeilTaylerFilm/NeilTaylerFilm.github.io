## TL;DR 
The Canon R6 III is near perfect camera. It has become my preferred camera for all my photography and videography needs. CLOG2 and Open Gate are the headline features of this camera release (in my opinion). This camera will handle anything you throw at it and do it well. Any complaints I have are really just nitpicks. 

## Context
My main camera beforehand was the Canon R5. It was left to me by my father in his will, and because of that, selling it has never been a consideration. It holds a lot of sentimental value for me. This left me with a problem.

I was preparing to spend five months travelling around Southeast Asia at the start of 2026. Taking an expensive camera halfway across the world is one thing. Taking one that you could never meaningfully replace if it were stolen, damaged or lost is another.

## Why Did I Pick Up The Canon R6 III?
So, as soon as I had watched every single launch review of the Canon R6 III, I decided to lock in my pre-order. I justified my purchase because of the sentimental attachment to the R5 but truthfully, Canon added a lot of features that actually got me excited to be using the camera everyday for the next five months.

It's my personal belief that photography has largely been solved for a while. If I was solely a photographer, I would feel more than comfortable using gear from five years ago. Sure, newer cameras might have slightly better autofocus and can eek out a little more dynamic range, but we're entering the realm of diminishing returns. However, I studied filmmaking. Video is an ever-increasing part of the content I capture. This is what made the R6 III enticing. It seemed with this release that Canon listened to their customers and added several video features that would've normally been reserved for the cinema line.

So, after five months of carrying the R6 III through heat, humidity, dust, dirt, rain, cities and more streets photography walks than I can remember, what did I ultimately think of the camera?

## I Cannot Believe The Video Features
### A Quick History Recap
The reason I couldn't believe the video features of the R6 III were real at first was because they sounded too good to be true. Historically, Canon has been quite reluctant to add features that users have asked for. The camera community often jokes about the "Canon Cripple Hammer" but it is partially true, Canon often reserved specific features for their cinema cameras because they knew if you wanted them that badly, you would pay more for the privilege. [Cripple Hammer History](https://youtu.be/hf8QtDE6B-E?si=KHOeBZZhHx4VnvRR&t=202 "cite")

For example, when the Canon R5 first released, it was only capable of C-Log. With subsequent firmware updates, Canon added C-Log3. Withholding their best log profile, C-Log2 for their cinema cameras. Meanwhile, Sony was putting their best log profile, S-Log3, in even their entry-level cameras.

In the last few years, Canon has lost a lot of the creator community to Sony. Both Sony and Canon claim they are the number one mirrorless camera brand, which lawyers on both sides can probably argue is true if you're looking at one specific metric. Regardless, if you keep up with the camera youtuber space, you’ll see that Sony is usually the brand of choice. [Why EVERYONE Is Switching To SONY - Jason Morris](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QZ969jsaeeo "cite")

Canon undoubtedly must have noticed this trend because the R6 III includes many features often talked about in the creator community, such as; open gate, CLOG2, waveform monitoring, false colour. Just to name a few.

### Why Is C-Log2 So Important?
As I mentioned earlier, C-Log2 is Canon's best log profile that was historically reserved for more expensive cameras. Canon recently became more lenient with putting C-Log2 into their cameras. We saw it first appear on the Canon R5ii and the Canon R1 when they were released simultaneously, and now it’s on the R6 III.
