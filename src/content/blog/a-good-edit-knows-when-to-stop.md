There is a point in an edit when every new adjustment seems to make something better and something else worse. The rhythm changes. A useful hesitation disappears. A cut becomes smoother, but the moment loses its character. Knowing when to leave it alone is part of the work.

## Listen to the sequence

This is demonstration writing, not an account of a real project. It gives a text-only entry enough room to breathe, with no missing thumbnail or empty image box.

Play a sequence without stopping. Write down where your attention moves. Then play it again with your hands away from the keyboard. It is easier to hear the overall rhythm when every moment is not an invitation to make another change.

### Three useful questions

1. What does this moment need to communicate?
2. What does the viewer already understand?
3. Is the next cut adding something, or simply moving on?

> A pause is still a decision.

## Leave yourself a way back

Save a version before making a substantial change. Naming it clearly takes almost no time and makes comparison far less mysterious. A short note about the decision can be more useful than another number on the end of a filename.

The best version is not necessarily the newest one. Sometimes a fresh pass confirms what was already working.
